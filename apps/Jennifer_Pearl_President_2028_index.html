Why I’m Running

Public office should belong to the public.

I am running to defend, support, and represent the American people—especially families, workers, caregivers, veterans, small businesses, and children who feel unheard by parties, donors, lobbyists, and permanent insiders. My first loyalty is to the citizens. This campaign begins with a simple promise: decisions will be explained openly, conflicts will be disclosed, and public service will come before corporate influence, back-room deals, and political convenience.

Three Core Principles

A presidency built on service, integrity, and stewardship.

Pillar One

People Before Power

Every decision begins with one question: does this protect and strengthen ordinary people? Working families, caregivers, veterans, children, and small businesses must come before lobbyists, monopolies, and insider circles.

Pillar Two

Clean Hands Governance

Financial transparency, meaningful conflict-of-interest rules, plain-language explanations, and independent oversight should be basic expectations of public office. What cannot be justified openly does not belong in government.

Pillar Three

Guarding the Future

The next generation should not inherit preventable debt, damaged land, weakened institutions, unaffordable healthcare, and neglected schools. Leadership must think beyond the next election cycle.

Write-In Information

Voting rules should be clear, official, and accurate.

Write-in requirements vary by state and may change. Voters should always confirm current instructions through official state and local election authorities.

Campaign interest forms, supporter lists, or preliminary signatures are campaign-organizing tools. They do not replace official election procedures, ballot-access requirements, voter registration, or instructions issued by election officials.

How Write-In Voting Works

Official Election Information Required

Write-in voting rules are established by each state and may also involve local election authorities.

Before voting, confirm whether a write-in candidate must file in advance, how the candidate’s name must be entered, whether write-in votes are counted in that jurisdiction, and what identification or registration requirements apply.

Use current information from an official Secretary of State, state elections division, county election office, or other legally authorized election authority. Campaign summaries should never replace official instructions.
